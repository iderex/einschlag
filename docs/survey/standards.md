# What the documentation standards require, and what they say about uncertainty

Two questions are asked of each document here. What does it require an operator
to record at a shooting scene, because that fixes what this tool can expect as
input. And what does it say about stating an uncertainty or a range, because that
is the gap this project claims to fill, and the claim is worth checking rather
than assuming.

## How these entries were obtained

The ASB documents and the ENFSI guideline are published free of charge and were
downloaded and read directly. The ISO 21043 parts are sold, and only the proof
preview of part 3 was reachable, which carries the front matter and the table of
contents and blanks the normative text. Where an entry says a clause was not
obtained, it means the text was not read, not that the clause is absent.

## Entries

### ANSI/ASB Standard 196, Standard for the Documentation and Processing of Shooting Scenes

First edition, 2026. ASB approved January 2026, ANSI approved March 2026. Issued
by the AAFS Standards Board, drafted by the Crime Scene Investigation and
Reconstruction Subcommittee of OSAC. Stable URL
<https://www.aafs.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/196_Std_e1.pdf>, listed
on the OSAC standards library at
<https://www.nist.gov/osac/standards-library/ansiasb-standard-196-26>.

Read in full, clause 1 through clause 6 and the front matter. The whole normative
text is ten pages.

What it requires to be documented for an impact. Clause 4.3.1.1 requires all
projectile impacts to be documented to include photography, labeling,
measurements and location. Clause 4.3.2 requires a unique identifier per impact,
recorded in photographs, notes and sketches, and allows impacts associated with a
single path to be documented in a corresponding manner such as A1, A2, A3. Clause
4.3.4 lists the characteristics to be documented, including the target surface
material, its contour as flat, convex or concave, and its angle; the size of the
impact as width and length; whether the impact is non-penetrating, penetrating or
perforating; and features such as pinch point, lead-in mark and bullet wipe.
Clause 4.3.5.1 requires the location of each impact to be measured using a
coordinate system that is clearly defined and recorded.

What it requires to be documented for a trajectory. Clause 4.4.1 requires the
directionality, impact sites and angle measurements to be recorded when
practicable. Clause 4.4.2.2.1 states that single impacts can provide trajectory
information through measurements to calculate an angle of impact using
trigonometric functions, and note 2 under clause 4.4.3 states that this is done
when the length and width of the impact are measured. Clause 4.4.2.2.2 covers two
or more impacts on one projectile, connected with rods, strings or lasers. Clause
4.4.3 requires impact angles to be measured by at least one of a listed set of
methods, and for the horizontal and vertical angle option it states that angles
are commonly reported to a whole degree, that the zero is defined and documented,
that vertical angles can be measured with an inclinometer, angle finder or
zero-edge protractor, and that horizontal angles can be measured with a zero-edge
protractor.

What it says about uncertainty. Nothing. There is no requirement, and no
recommendation, to state an uncertainty, an error, a range, a tolerance or a
confidence on an impact angle or on a trajectory. The nearest thing in the
document is the reporting convention in clause 4.4.3 a) 1), that angles are
commonly reported to a whole degree, which fixes a resolution and says nothing
about how far the value may be from the truth. The words uncertainty, error,
tolerance and confidence do not appear as requirements anywhere in clauses 1
through 6.

### ANSI/ASB Standard 159, Standard for Scene Investigation and Reconstruction, Foundational Principles

First edition, 2024. ASB approved July 2022, ANSI approved January 2024. Issued
by the AAFS Standards Board. Stable URL
<https://www.aafs.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/159_Std_e1.pdf>. Cited
normatively by ANSI/ASB 196 clause 4.1.2, which requires it to be used in
conjunction with that document.

Read in full. Four pages of normative text plus an informative bibliography.

What it requires. Clause 4.4 requires scene practitioners to use scientifically
reliable and valid methods, practices and analytical procedures based on best
practices, peer-reviewed studies or validated techniques prior to use on scenes.
Clause 4.5 requires the scene to be documented so that the context of the
evidence is preserved. Clause 4.7 requires the practitioner to document their
actions, to refrain from tasks they are not qualified for, and to document
intentional deviations and, on discovery, unintentional deviations, errors and
omissions. Clause 4.1 requires a deviation from the document to be documented and
explained.

What it says about uncertainty. Nothing. Clause 4.7 d) requires an error to be
documented once discovered, which is a record of a mistake and not a statement of
the uncertainty of a measurement. The document has no requirement to express an
uncertainty or a range on any quantity.

### ANSI/ASB Standard 096, Standard Method for the Examination and Documentation of Ammunition and Ammunition Components

First edition, 2022. ASB approved September 2021, ANSI approved February 2022.
Stable URL
<https://www.aafs.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/096_Std_e1.pdf>.

Read in part: the front matter, the table of contents, clause 1 scope, clause 3
and clause 4.1. Its subject is the examination of ammunition and ammunition
components by a firearm and toolmark examiner, and it excludes the microscopic
comparison of toolmarks. It was read because it appeared in the same search and
was checked for relevance. It does not govern scene geometry and it produces
nothing this tool takes as input. It is recorded here so that the reading is
visible rather than being an unexplained absence.

What it says about uncertainty. Not evaluated. The clauses that could carry such
a requirement, 4.6 and 4.7, were not read, because the document had already been
established as out of scope for this project's input.

### ENFSI Guideline for Evaluative Reporting in Forensic Science

Approved version 3.0, foreword dated 8 March 2015, published by the European
Network of Forensic Science Institutes under the STEOFRAE project with EU
funding. Stable URL <https://enfsi.eu/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/m1_guideline.pdf>.

Read in part: the foreword, clause 1 scope and clause 2 evaluative reporting. The
worked case examples and the audit template were not read.

What it requires to be documented. Nothing about a scene. This is a reporting
guideline, not a scene documentation standard, and it is in this survey because
it is the document in the European landscape that speaks most directly to
expressing uncertainty.

What it says about uncertainty. Clause 2.3 states that evaluation of forensic
science findings in court uses probability as a measure of uncertainty, based on
the findings, associated data and expert knowledge, case specific propositions
and conditioning information. Clause 2.4 states that evaluation is based on the
assignment of a likelihood ratio, that the framework applies to all forensic
science disciplines, and that for the evaluative part only a likelihood ratio
based approach is considered. Clause 1.3 states that practitioners will not
report on matters outside their own area of expertise.

This is a requirement to express uncertainty, and it is not the same requirement
this project's gap is about. It governs the strength of findings weighed against
competing propositions. It does not require a measured geometric quantity, such
as an angle of incidence or a reconstructed position, to carry an uncertainty.

### ISO 21043-3:2025, Forensic sciences, Part 3, Analysis

First edition, 2025, ISO/TC 272 in collaboration with CEN/TC 419. Catalogue entry
<https://www.iso.org/standard/72040.html>. The proof preview used here is
<https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/72040/5ae0d94d087242cd98b3ff4459b87e90/ISO-PRF-21043-3.pdf>.

Read in part, and the part read is small. The preview carries the cover, the
foreword, the introduction and the table of contents. The normative text is
blanked in the preview and was not obtained; the standard is sold and no copy was
available on this route.

What was verified from the table of contents. Clause 4.7 is titled "Measurement
uncertainty and significant figures". Clause 10.4 is titled "Reconstruction".
Clause 11 is titled "Reliability of observations". The document is applicable to
all disciplines of forensic science.

What it says about uncertainty. Not obtained. A clause exists whose title names
measurement uncertainty, and what it requires is exactly the thing this survey
would most want to quote. Quoting a requirement from a clause title would be an
assertion this project has not read, so it is not made here. This is the largest
open item in this file.

### Other parts of ISO 21043, and other documents sought

ISO 21043-1 vocabulary, ISO 21043-2 recovery, transport and storage, ISO 21043-4
interpretation and ISO 21043-5 reporting were not obtained. Part 4 and part 5 are
the parts most likely to carry a requirement about expressing uncertainty in what
reaches a court, alongside part 3. Note that ANSI/ASB 159's bibliography cites
ISO 21043-1:2018(E), while the current catalogue listing for part 1 is a 2025
edition; which edition a given laboratory works to was not established here.

ASTM Practice E620-18 is named in ANSI/ASB 196 clause 5.2 as where guidance on
report preparation may be found. It was not obtained.

ISO/IEC 17025 was not obtained. It is the accreditation standard under which many
of the laboratories in the intended audience operate, and one of the studies in
the ellipse-accuracy survey states that the need to quantify measurement
uncertainty for accreditation was the reason its work was undertaken. Whether
17025 imposes a requirement that reaches a reconstructed trajectory angle is not
answered here.

## Whether any standard read requires an uncertainty to be stated

Of the documents read in full, neither requires it.

ANSI/ASB Standard 196 is the standard that governs exactly the measurements this
tool takes as input, and it requires an impact angle to be measured and recorded
without requiring anything to be said about how far that value may be from the
truth. It sets a reporting resolution of a whole degree and stops there. ANSI/ASB
Standard 159, the foundational document it sits on, requires reliable and valid
methods and requires errors to be documented once found, and imposes no
requirement to state an uncertainty on a measurement.

That is the evidence for the gap this project exists to fill, and it is a reading
of two documents rather than a claim about a landscape. It is bounded in three
ways and each bound matters.

The ENFSI guideline does require probability as the measure of uncertainty, for
evaluative reporting of findings against competing propositions. So a practice
that assigns a likelihood ratio to a reconstruction is already inside a written
requirement to express uncertainty, and this project should not describe the
field as one where nobody is required to say anything.

ISO 21043-3:2025 has a clause titled "Measurement uncertainty and significant
figures" whose text was not obtained, and until it is read the honest statement
is that the question is open for that standard rather than answered negatively.

And this survey read the North American consensus standards and one European
guideline. National requirements, laboratory accreditation requirements and the
requirements applying in the jurisdictions where much of the intended audience
works were not read.

The claim this file supports, therefore, is narrow and it is the one worth
having. The standard that specifies what an operator writes down about a bullet
impact and a trajectory does not ask for an uncertainty, so a tool that takes
that documentation as input has to be able to accept a measurement with no stated
uncertainty and must decide what to do about it, and that decision belongs in the
input-format record rather than in a default buried in a parser.
